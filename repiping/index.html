Signs your supply lines are near the end

A single leak is a repair. A pattern is a repipe discussion. Pinhole leaks in copper, repeated repairs on the same run, discolored water that comes from every tap and pressure that has dropped over the years all point to pipes that are wearing out from the inside.

Callers who describe repipe needs usually mention a leak that was fixed and another that appeared a few months later. That second leak is the signal. When repairs begin to cost more than a planned replacement, the repipe becomes the cheaper path. Our leak detection service can confirm whether the problem is isolated or widespread before you commit.

Warning signs that point to repiping

Repeated pinhole leaks

More than one in a short span means the pipe wall is thin across the run.

Discolored water from several fixtures

Brown or orange water can mean internal corrosion in galvanized or older steel lines.

Falling pressure

Mineral buildup or corrosion narrows the pipe bore over time.

Visible green or white crust on pipes

Surface corrosion at joints and fittings often matches wall damage inside.

Material with a known problem history

Some pipe types used in past decades have a record of failure. A plumber should identify what you have.

Why Camarillo homes come up for repiping

Camarillo incorporated in 1964, and its population rose from about 2,400 in 1960 to over 52,000 by 1990. Most of the housing is therefore between 30 and 60 years old. Homes in that age range have supply lines that are decades into their service life, and some builders in those years used pipe materials that later showed problems.

We identify the material during the walkthrough and tell you what the record is for that type.

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. Hard water leaves mineral scale inside pipes and on fittings and narrows the bore over time. Pressure drops are common in old lines because of that buildup. The effect on a repipe decision is practical. If the system is scaled and leaking, replacing the supply is a cleaner fix than patching.

Copper, PEX and what we recommend

Both materials are accepted for residential supply lines. The right pick depends on the house and your budget.

Permits, inspections and wall repair

The City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division lists copper re-pipe among the permit types that can be applied for online, and its office number is (805) 388-5395. For other materials or partial work, ask the city which permit applies to your project before work begins. An inspection checks the new piping before the walls are closed.

Wall repair is part of the plan. A repipe through a slab-on-grade house can run overhead and through attic spaces, which limits floor damage. A two-story home often uses closets and chases for vertical runs. We explain the cut plan before we start and tell you who handles drywall and paint.

A repipe is a planned project, and insurance rarely pays for it unless a sudden covered event caused the damage. For your price, call (805) 249-7135.

Code, listings and inspection for a repipe

A repipe follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which requires pipe and fittings listed for potable water, support at set intervals and a pressure test. The city inspects the work before walls close, so that is the point at which any problem gets caught. Ask us to walk you through the inspection date and what the inspector will check.
