What hydro jetting does that a cable cannot

A cable opens a channel through the blockage. It leaves the grease, scale and debris on the pipe wall, and the line narrows again. A jetter pushes a hose with a specialized nozzle through the pipe and sprays water backward and forward at high pressure. The spray scours the walls, flushes the debris to the main and cuts through roots that a cable tends to push aside.

We use jetting after a cable pass when the cause is grease, mineral buildup or fine roots. We do not use it blindly. If a camera inspection shows a cracked or collapsed pipe, jetting can make the damage worse, and the right answer is sewer line repair.

Main line backing up: when jetting is the right call

Repeat clogs on the same line

If a cable clears it for a month and then it clogs again, buildup on the wall is the usual cause.

Kitchen lines in older homes

Decades of grease coat the pipe. Jetting restores the full inside diameter.

Fine root intrusion

Jetting trims hair-like roots at the joints, though a repair is needed if roots keep returning.

Restaurants and food service

Grease lines in commercial kitchens need regular scouring, as covered on our commercial plumbing page.

Why jetting matters in a city that grew in waves

Camarillo grew quickly from the 1960s through the 1990s, and many of those homes still run the original clay, cast iron or early plastic lateral. Older pipe has more joints, and joints invite roots. Under the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, the owner maintains the side sewer that serves the building, so a recurring clog is an owner expense, not a district one.

The same code lists limits on what can enter the sewer, including a limit on dispersed grease, oil and fat of 600 parts per million, and a requirement that food service businesses control grease. For homeowners, the practical takeaway is simple. Grease poured down a kitchen drain does not leave the pipe. It settles and hardens, and jetting is how we get it out.

How a jetting visit runs

A safe jetting job follows an order. These are the four stages.

Inspect

We locate the cleanout and, when possible, camera the line first.

Open

A cable pass opens the path if the line is fully blocked.

Jet

The nozzle scours the full length at a pressure suited to the pipe material.

Verify

We run water, check flow and recheck with the camera if needed.

Safety and prep before jetting

Pressure work in a pipe is safe when the pipe is sound and the area is ready.

  • Clear the area around the cleanout so the hose can be set up.
  • Tell us about any past repairs, patches or lining on the line.
  • Keep children and pets away while the hose is in use.
  • Do not run water or flush during the work.
  • Move vehicles from the driveway if the cleanout is near it.

Limits we are honest about

Jetting is not a repair. If a pipe has a crack, a belly or a collapsed section, jetting only moves debris around. A pipe that is pitch-fiber, thin-walled or already lined needs a lower pressure or a different method. We confirm material and condition before we pick a pressure. Where a lined section exists, we ask for the paperwork.

We also do not promise that a line will stay clear forever. Roots return where the pipe leaks at a joint. Grease returns if the kitchen habit continues. Our job is to clear the line, explain why it clogged and tell you what would prevent a repeat. For plain clogs without a recurring pattern, our drain cleaning team starts with a cable.

Equipment ratings and sewer rules

Jetting equipment has a rated pressure and a hose and nozzle matched to the pipe size, and we follow the maker's instructions for each. According to the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, owners keep their side sewers, and Section 6002 limits what may be discharged. A camera before and after the cleaning records the result against that standard.
