What Oxnard's own water report says about hardness

The City of Oxnard's 2023 water quality report gives a supply mix of 32.8 percent Calleguas imported water, 33 percent United Water groundwater and 34.2 percent city groundwater. Hardness ranged from 103 to 557 ppm and averaged 319.4 ppm. The city disinfects with chloramine. For questions about water quality, the city's Water Division number is (805) 385-8136.

Hardness at that level leaves mineral scale in water heaters, on fixtures and inside older pipes. Tank heaters in hard water areas often need a flush and anode inspection to reach a normal life, and tankless units need descaling.

Our water softener and filtration page covers the options, and our tankless water heater page covers descaling. Your tap can differ from the citywide average, so check your own supplier's latest report.

Sewer laterals in Oxnard: who is responsible

The City of Oxnard states that the private sewer lateral, including the connection to the public sewer in the right-of-way, is the property owner's responsibility. The city's Wastewater Division is reachable at (805) 271-2200 for questions about the public line. If your main line is backing up, the first question is whether the blockage is in your lateral or in the public main.

A camera inspection answers that question. A sewer camera inspection shows roots, breaks, bellies and buildup, and it gives you video to share with the city if the problem sits in the public line. If the lateral itself is damaged, sewer line repair covers spot repairs, lining and replacement. For grease and root buildup in a sound line, hydro jetting clears the pipe wall.

What to do before we arrive in Oxnard

A few minutes of prep makes the first visit faster.

Find and close the shutoff

The main valve is near the meter or at the front of the house. Close it if water is spreading.

Stop using the drains

If sewage is backing up, do not run the sink, shower or washer. It adds to the overflow.

Clear the way

Move belongings off wet floors and clear a path to the water heater, the cleanout or the leak.

Take photos

Document damage before cleanup in case you file an insurance claim.

Note the meter reading

Writing it down with all fixtures off helps us tell a leak from normal use.
