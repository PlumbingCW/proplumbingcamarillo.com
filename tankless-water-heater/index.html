How a tankless heater differs from a tank

A tankless unit heats water only when a tap is open. Water passes through a heat exchanger, a gas burner or electric element lights, and hot water comes out until the tap closes. There is no tank to fill, so there is no standby heat loss and no running out in the middle of a long shower.

The tradeoff is that the unit needs the right supply. Gas models need a gas line sized for the burner, a proper vent and clearance. Electric models draw a lot of current and often need a dedicated circuit. A tankless unit that is wrong for the gas supply will shut off or flicker under load, no matter how good the unit is.

Why tankless units fail in hard water areas

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm in its annual report. That is hard enough to scale a tankless heat exchanger steadily.

Scale on the heat exchanger

Minerals bake onto the narrow passages. Flow drops, temperatures swing and the unit throws an error code.

Clogged inlet screen

Sediment collects in the filter at the cold inlet and cuts flow.

Error codes after long use

A unit that has not been flushed in years often reports an exchanger or flow fault.

Water that runs hot then cold

Scale can make the unit overshoot and shut down for safety.

Flush and descale: what it is and when

Descaling means circulating a mild acid solution, usually white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved descaler, through the heat exchanger with a small pump, then rinsing. The job takes about an hour and brings back flow and temperature stability. Many makers recommend doing it yearly in hard water, and they print the interval in the manual.

We also clean the inlet filter, check the gas pressure, test the vent and read stored error codes. If you bought the unit years ago and have never had it serviced, expect scale. A unit with isolation valves installed makes the flush much easier, and we can add them during the visit if yours are missing.

Repairs, parts and honest limits

We repair flow sensors, igniters, fans, thermistors and control boards on common brands. Before we replace parts, we confirm whether scale or a supply problem is the real cause, because a new board will not fix a clogged heat exchanger. If the exchanger is cracked or the unit is past its useful life, we say so and quote replacement.

Some manufacturers limit warranty support to certified installers, so keep your paperwork. For a failed tank unit that you want to convert, see water heater installation for the planning steps.

Softening and scale control for tankless owners

The simplest way to protect a tankless unit in Camarillo is to treat the water before it arrives. A whole-house softener or a scale-reduction cartridge on the cold line lowers the mineral load on the heat exchanger. Our water softener and filtration page explains the options.

Water softener salt, descaler and a service schedule add some cost. They save more than they cost if the alternative is replacing a heat exchanger early. We help owners price the options before the next failure.
