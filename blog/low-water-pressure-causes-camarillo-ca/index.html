Low Water Pressure in Camarillo, CA: Causes and Fixes

.

Weak flow at one tap is a small fix. Weak flow everywhere is a system problem. This guide shows how to measure your pressure, narrow down the cause and decide what to repair first.

Start with one fixture or the whole house

Ask the first question before you touch anything. Is the pressure low at one faucet, at hot water only or throughout the house? One faucet points to a clogged aerator or a bad cartridge. Hot water only points to the heater or the hot line. The whole house points to the main line, the pressure regulator or the supply itself.

Then measure. A pressure gauge screws onto a hose bib and costs little. Read it with no water running. Most homes are happy between about 50 and 70 psi. Below 40 psi feels weak, and above 80 psi is hard on fixtures. A reading that swings from high to low during the day points to supply variation. A steady low reading points to the regulator or a restriction.

Common causes by location

Where the problem shows up tells you where to look.

One faucet weak

A clogged aerator, a worn cartridge or a partly closed stop valve.

One shower weak

Mineral scale in the showerhead or a failing mixing valve.

Hot side weak

Sediment in the water heater or a partly closed valve on the heater.

Whole house weak

A failing pressure regulator, a partly closed main valve or a leak in the line.

Pressure falls over years

Scale or corrosion narrowing old pipes. See repiping.

Sudden drop

A burst pipe or main line leak. See burst pipe repair.

The regulator and the main valve

A pressure reducing valve, often a bell shaped brass fitting near the main, steps street pressure down to a safe level. They wear out. A failing regulator may let pressure creep up or drop and stay low. Many can be adjusted with a screw on top, and many are simply replaced when old.

The main valve at the house is also a suspect. A gate valve that is only partly open throttles the flow, and old gate valves often fail to open fully. Turning it a quarter turn may help, but do so gently because old valves can break.

A ball valve upgrade is a modest job and gives you a reliable shutoff in an emergency. Our water line repair team checks both on a visit.

Scale, hard water and old pipe

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness of 217.6 ppm on average, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. Hard water leaves scale inside pipes and fixtures, and the buildup narrows the bore over the years. Aerators and showerheads show it first, because their small holes clog quickly.

You can soak an aerator or a showerhead in vinegar for an hour to dissolve scale. If pressure is low in an older home even after that, galvanized steel and old copper lines may be scaled inside.

A repipe restores flow, and our repiping page explains the choice between partial and whole house work. A softener cuts scale on new pipe, and our water softener and filtration page walks through it.

This takes about 20 minutes and answers most questions.

Hot water only, or one bathroom only

If cold water is strong and hot water is weak, look at the water heater. Sediment can clog the outlet, a valve on the heater may be partly closed, or a hot line may be scaled. A flush often helps, and so does checking the shutoff valves on the heater.

If only one bathroom is weak, look at the stop valves under the sink and at the shower cartridge, since those parts wear out and scale up.

A whole house filter or a softener can also restrict flow when its cartridge is dirty or the unit is sized too small. If the pressure dropped right after a filter change or a softener install, check the bypass valve and the filter housing. These are simple to inspect, and our water softener and filtration team can service them.

When high pressure is the real problem

Some homes have the opposite problem. Pressure above about 80 psi strains fixtures, causes banging pipes and wears out washer hoses and toilet fill valves. A failed regulator can send full street pressure into the house. A gauge on a hose bib tells you quickly, and replacing a regulator is usually a short job. We read the pressure on every service call for that reason.

Local note: water sources and pressure in Camarillo

The City of Camarillo supply is about 26 percent well water from four city wells in the Fox Canyon Aquifer and about 74 percent imported Calleguas Municipal Water District water, according to its 2021 report. Other parts of the area are served by Camrosa Water District, whose supply is about half imported and half groundwater.

Street pressure varies with the elevation of your lot and the supplier's system. If your pressure has changed and the house is not the cause, the Water Division at (805) 388-5373 can say whether work is happening nearby.

Source: City of Camarillo 2021 water quality report and Camrosa Water District public information.
