What an anode rod does inside your tank

A tank water heater is a steel vessel with a glass-like lining. Tiny gaps in that lining expose bare steel to hot water, and hot water corrodes steel quickly. The anode rod hangs inside the tank and is made of a metal, usually magnesium or aluminum, that corrodes more readily than steel. Water attacks the rod first, so the tank wall is spared.

The rod is a consumable part. It wears away over the years, and once it is mostly gone, the corrosion moves to the tank itself. A tank that has lost its rod early in its life tends to leak sooner than one whose rod was checked and replaced.

For that reason a rod inspection is one of the lowest cost ways to extend the life of a heater that is otherwise sound.

Why Camarillo water can use up a rod faster

The City of Camarillo reports that its well water averages 217.6 ppm hardness, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. About 26 percent of the city supply comes from four city wells in the Fox Canyon Aquifer. The rest is Calleguas Municipal Water District imported water, which the same report lists at 122 ppm for Jensen plant water and 272 ppm for locally stored surface water that Calleguas treated.

Hardness is not the same thing as corrosiveness, so we do not claim that hard water eats rods. What it does is coat the rod and the tank bottom with scale, and a scaled rod can hide how much metal is left.

A rod that looks thick may be a thin core under a crust of mineral. For that reason we check by feel and by scraping, not just by looking, and we flush the tank at the same visit when sediment is heavy.

Signs the rod may be used up

Some of these signs overlap with other heater problems, so treat them as a reason to inspect.

Rotten egg smell from hot water

Some bacteria react with a magnesium rod and produce a sulfur smell. A smell from the hot side only is a clue.

Popping or rumbling from the tank

Sediment under the burner or element can make noise. It often goes with an old rod.

Rust colored hot water

Rust from the tank interior shows up in hot water only. Cold water stays clear.

A heater past its sixth or seventh year with no service

A tank that has never been checked is the best candidate.

Water pooling at the base

A leak from the tank body means the damage is done. A rod will not fix a leaking tank.

How a replacement works

The job takes an hour or so on a tank in good access. The hard part is usually breaking the old fitting loose.

Shut down the heater

Turn off the gas control or the breaker and close the cold water supply valve at the tank.

Relieve pressure

Open a hot water tap in the house and drain a few gallons from the tank so the rod can come out without spraying.

Loosen the rod

The rod head is a hex fitting on top of the tank. It is often tight, so we use a long breaker bar and a socket.

Check and replace

We pull the rod, measure what is left and fit a new one with thread sealant. The clearance above the heater matters here.

Refill and test

We refill the tank, bleed the air, check the fitting for drips and restore power or gas.

Clearance problems and flexible rods

A standard rod is about as long as the tank is tall. Many heaters in Camarillo garages and closets sit under a shelf or a low ceiling, which leaves little room to pull a rod straight up.

A segmented or flexible rod bends in the middle and goes in with less headroom. It is a useful fix when clearance is tight, and it costs a little more than a solid rod.

On a heater that is leaking or has an old tank, replacing the rod rarely pays off. When the tank itself has failed, see water heater replacement and have a plan before the leak spreads. When the heater is sound but noisy or slow, a water heater repair visit can check the rod, the elements or the burner together.

How often to check the rod

Many plumbers suggest looking at the rod every few years, and more often in hard water. A reasonable plan is to inspect it around year three or four and again every two or three years after that. If the rod is down to the core wire, replace it. If it is still thick, put the cap back on and recheck later.
