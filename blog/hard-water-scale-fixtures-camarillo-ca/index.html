Hard Water Scale on Fixtures in Camarillo, CA

.

White crust on faucets, cloudy glass and slow showerheads all come from the same source: dissolved minerals that stay behind when water dries. This guide shows how to clean scale safely, how to prevent it and when a softener is a good idea.

Why scale forms

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. When water dries on a surface, the minerals stay behind as white or gray crust. Heat speeds the process, so scale builds fastest in water heaters, kettles, showerheads and kitchen faucets. The result looks bad, shortens the life of fixtures and cuts flow.

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. Divide by 17.1 for grains per gallon, which gives roughly 12.7 for the average. The Calleguas treated surface water was reported at 272 ppm, or about 15.9 grains per gallon. Scale shows up in a matter of weeks in these conditions.

How to clean scale from a faucet or showerhead

Vinegar works for most cases and is gentle on the plumbing.

Check the finish

Some finishes, such as brass, brushed nickel and oil rubbed bronze, can be damaged by acid. Read the maker's notes first.

Soak in white vinegar

Fill a bag with vinegar, tie it around the fixture and leave it for an hour or so.

Scrub gently

Use a soft brush or a toothbrush on holes and edges.

Rinse and wipe dry

Rinse with water and dry with a soft cloth to avoid new spots.

Clear the aerator

Unscrew it, soak it and clear the screen. Reinstall it.

What scale does beyond looks

Scale is cosmetic at first and mechanical later.

Cuts flow at showerheads and aerators

Small holes clog first.

Wears faucet cartridges

Grit scrapes seals and shortens their life.

Insulates heater elements and tank bottoms

The heater works harder and wears out sooner.

Stains glass and tile

A permanent film can etch glass over time.

Clogs appliance valves

Dishwashers, washers and ice makers have small screens.

What to avoid

Do not mix cleaners. Vinegar and bleach produce harmful fumes. Do not use harsh acids on natural stone, such as marble, because they etch it. Do not scrape chrome with abrasives, because it scratches. Skip drain gels and aggressive descalers in lines that feed an older water heater, unless the product says it is safe for your setup.

If a faucet leaks after cleaning, the scale may have been hiding a worn cartridge. That is a repair, not a cleaning problem, and our faucet and sink repair page has the details. For a toilet that runs because scale holds the flapper open, see toilet repair.

When a softener makes sense

If you spend a lot of time scrubbing, replace aerators often or see a short life for water heaters, a whole house softener is worth a price check. It protects fixtures, appliances and pipes, and it improves how soap lathers. It does need salt and a drain connection, and some people dislike the feel of softened water.

Our water softener and filtration page explains the options in more detail. A drinking water filter is a separate product with a different job. If your water heater is tankless, see our tankless water heater page for descaling. We serve Camarillo and nearby towns, and the service areas page lists them.

Showers, glass and tile

Shower glass shows scale as a cloudy film that wipes off easily at first and etches over time. A squeegee after each shower removes most of the water before it dries. Once a film has formed, a vinegar and water spray left on for 15 minutes loosens it.

Rinse well and avoid mixing it with other cleaners. Natural stone tile needs a stone safe cleaner, because acids can dull the surface.

Showerheads clog from the inside. Unscrew the head, soak it in vinegar and poke the holes with a pin. A head that stays clogged after a soak may have scale inside the stem. A shower that runs hot and cold, or one that loses flow, can also have a worn cartridge in the valve, which is a repair rather than a cleaning job.

Dishwashers, washers and other appliances

Appliances with small screens and valves feel scale quickly. A dishwasher that leaves spots may need a rinse aid and an occasional cleaning cycle with a dishwasher cleaner or vinegar, depending on the maker. A washing machine inlet screen can clog and slow the fill. Check the screens where the hoses connect, with the water off, and rinse out any grit.

The water heater collects the most scale of any appliance because heat drives minerals out of the water. A tank that has never been flushed may have a thick layer, and our water heater repair page explains what we do about it. Many homes pair a softener with a yearly check, and both choices cut the time you spend cleaning.

How hardness is measured and reported

Water hardness is reported in parts per million, or in grains per gallon, with 1 grain per gallon equal to about 17.1 ppm. According to the City of Camarillo 2021 report, well water averaged 217.6 ppm, which is about 12.7 grains per gallon. Standards for descriptions vary, so compare numbers rather than labels when you shop for a softener.

Local note: why Camarillo water is hard

Camarillo gets about 26 percent of its water from four city wells in the Fox Canyon Aquifer, and about 74 percent from Calleguas Municipal Water District imported water, per the City of Camarillo 2021 water report. Groundwater picks up minerals as it moves through rock and soil, which is why well water tends to be harder.

The mix at your tap varies by where you live. The City of Camarillo Water Division at (805) 388-5373 can say which source serves your address, and your bill names your supplier.
