Sewer Cleanout in Camarillo, CA: Where to Find It

.

A sewer cleanout is a capped pipe that gives a plumber direct access to your sewer line. Knowing where yours is can turn a flooded house into a short service call. This guide shows where to look, what the Camarillo Sanitary District code says and what to do in a backup.

What a cleanout is

A cleanout is a vertical or angled pipe with a threaded cap that sits at ground level or just below it. It connects to your private sewer line, the lateral, and gives a plumber a place to feed a cable or a camera without pulling a toilet or cutting into the pipe.

It also lets sewage rise and overflow outdoors in a backup instead of inside the house, which is a real advantage.

Most cleanouts are made of ABS or PVC plastic in newer homes and cast iron or clay in older ones. They are often near the foundation at the front or side of the house, in a flowerbed, near a hose bib or at the property line. The cap is often white, black or green and can be hidden under landscaping.

What the Camarillo Sanitary District code says

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code addresses the side sewer in several places. Section 4107 says the owner maintains the side sewer, including the lateral. Section 4105 requires a cleanout. Section 4108 requires a written permit to build a lateral. The code has the full text, and we suggest you confirm the current wording with the district.

What that means in practice is that the line from your house to the public main is your responsibility, and a cleanout is the way to reach it. If you are building or replacing a lateral, ask the district about a permit before work begins. Our sewer line repair team can walk through the steps with you.

How to find your cleanout

Look for a round cap, often with a square nut or a screw top.

Some homes have an indoor cleanout, often in a wall or the floor.

The pipe that exits near the lowest toilet or the water heater often leads to it.

What to do in a backup

First, stop using water. Do not flush, run the sink or start the washer. Then find the cleanout. If you can reach it, remove the cap slowly with the right wrench. Standing sewage may rise in the pipe. If it flows out, the blockage is likely between the cleanout and the public main. If the pipe stays empty, the blockage is between the cleanout and the house.

Wear gloves and protect your skin. Do not use drain chemicals in a backed up line, because they can burn you when a plumber later opens it. Then call. We use a cable or hydro jet from the cleanout, and a camera shows what we are dealing with. See sewer line cleaning for what a main line clearing involves.

No cleanout? Add one

If your home has no cleanout, a plumber can add one. It involves cutting into the lateral outside or inside the house and installing a fitting with a cap. It is a modest project and well worth doing, because it cuts the cost of every future service call. The code requires one, and our sewer camera inspection page shows how access helps a camera run.

If a cleanout is cracked, buried or missing a cap, raise the issue early. A missing cap lets roots, rain and debris enter the line. For homes in nearby cities, the rules differ. The City of Oxnard says its private laterals belong to the owner, and our Oxnard plumbing page covers that.

Caps, covers and safety

A cleanout cap should be tight, intact and easy to reach. A missing or cracked cap lets rain, dirt and pests into the sewer line and lets gas out. If your cap is buried under mulch or a lawn, ask a plumber to install a riser and a box cover so it stays at grade. Mark the spot on your property sketch so a future owner can find it.

Open a cleanout only when you must, and do it with care. A line that is backed up holds pressure, and removing the cap can release sewage quickly. Stand to one side, wear gloves and boots and keep children and pets away. If sewage reaches a floor inside the house, treat it as a health hazard and call a cleanup company in addition to the plumber.

Code references for cleanouts

The California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) sets where cleanouts must go, and the Camarillo Sanitary District requires one under Section 4105. Cleanouts should be the same size as the line they serve and accessible with a clear working space. According to the district code, the owner maintains the side sewer, so the cleanout is your access point.

Cleanouts in neighboring cities

Cleanout rules differ from city to city. Oxnard owners will find the city's lateral rules on our Oxnard plumbing page, and the areas page lists the other nearby towns we visit.

Local note: who owns what in Camarillo

The Camarillo Sanitary District code says the owner maintains the side sewer, including the lateral, and requires a cleanout. The public main in the street is the district's. When a backup occurs, a camera helps decide which section holds the blockage.

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code is the reference for permits and rules. If you are unsure whether your address is inside the district, check your bill or ask the City of Camarillo.

Source: Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, Sections 4105, 4107 and 4108.
