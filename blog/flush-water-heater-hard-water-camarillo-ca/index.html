How to Flush a Water Heater in Camarillo, CA

.

Sediment settles at the bottom of a tank heater and hard water adds more of it. A flush clears that layer and can quiet a noisy tank. Here is how it works, how often to do it in Camarillo and when to leave it alone.

Why sediment builds up in a tank

Hot water drops dissolved minerals out of solution. Calcium and magnesium fall to the bottom of the tank and form a layer. On a gas heater, that layer sits between the burner and the water. The burner has to heat through it, which wastes fuel and can overheat the tank bottom. On an electric heater, the lower element can get buried in scale and burn out.

The result is a heater that runs longer, makes popping or rumbling sounds and loses capacity. A flush drains water through the tank and carries part of that sediment out with it. It does not remove hard scale that has cemented, but it clears the loose layer and can add useful years to a tank in decent shape.

What the City of Camarillo report says about hardness

The City of Camarillo's 2021 report lists its well water at 217.6 ppm hardness on average, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. The Calleguas Jensen plant water was 122 ppm, and locally stored surface water that Calleguas treated was 272 ppm. Divide ppm by 17.1 to get grains per gallon, which makes the city well average about 12.7 gpg.

Water at that level is hard to very hard on common scales. A tank heater in hard water gathers sediment faster than one in soft water. That is why we suggest a yearly flush for Camarillo tanks that do not have a softener, and a check every two years for those that do. The right interval for your house depends on how much sediment comes out the first time.

Signs your tank has a sediment problem

A tank in this state needs attention, and a flush is the first step.

Popping or rumbling when the heater runs

Steam bubbles push through the layer and make noise.

Hot water that runs out faster than it used to

Sediment takes up volume in the tank.

Cloudy or gritty hot water

Particles come out with the flow.

Higher gas or power bills

The burner or element works longer to heat the same water.

A drain valve that clogs when you open it

Sediment plugs the valve and needs a tool to clear.

What to do to flush a tank safely

Treat the water as scalding. A flush is a simple job with a few cautions.

Turn off the heat

Set a gas heater to pilot or off and turn off the breaker for an electric heater. Never run an electric element dry.

Close the cold supply

Shut the valve above the tank so no new water comes in.

Attach a hose

Connect a garden hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or the yard.

Open a hot tap and the drain

Open a hot tap in the house to let air in, then open the drain valve. Hot water comes out, so be careful.

Flush until clear

Let it run. Open the cold supply briefly to stir the bottom. Close everything when the water runs clear.

Refill and restart

Close the drain valve, open the cold supply, let the tank fill with the hot tap open and then restore power or gas.

When not to flush

An old tank with a lot of scale can start to leak after a flush, because the sediment may be plugging a pinhole. That is not a reason to skip maintenance, but it is a reason to do it before the tank is near the end of its life.

If the heater is past a decade and has never been flushed, call a plumber and plan for a replacement. See water heater replacement for what to expect.

Do not flush if the drain valve is plastic and brittle, if the heater leaks, or if you smell gas. A plastic valve can snap, and a leaking tank needs service. A water heater repair visit can swap the valve for a brass one that is easier to use.

A softener changes the math

A softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium and sharply reduces scale. If you are tired of annual flushes, a softener is worth pricing, and our water softener and filtration page walks through the options. Softening also protects faucets, showerheads and appliances.

It does not remove the need for maintenance. Tanks still collect some sediment, and anode rods still wear. Plan a yearly look either way. Our team serves Camarillo and nearby towns, and you can see the area list on our service areas page.

Standards and maker instructions for flushing

Water heater makers publish flushing and service instructions, and those take priority over general guidance. The California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) covers relief valves and drains. A gas heater should also keep clear of anything flammable, so do not store paint or solvents near it.
