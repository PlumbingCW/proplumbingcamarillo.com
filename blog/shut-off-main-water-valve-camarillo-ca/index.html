How to Shut Off the Main Water Valve in Camarillo, CA

.

In a burst pipe, the time between the first spray and the shutoff decides the damage. Most homeowners cannot find the valve under stress. Learn where it is, how to turn it and how to test it now, while nothing is wrong.

Where the main shutoff usually sits

The main shutoff is the valve that controls all water into the house. In many Camarillo homes it sits on the outside wall facing the street, near the hose bib. In others it is in the garage, in a utility closet, by the water heater or in a basement or crawl space where one exists.

Look for a round wheel handle or a lever handle on a pipe coming through the wall or floor.

There is also a utility valve in the meter box near the street. That valve belongs to the utility, and homeowners should not use it unless the supplier says it is okay. A key or tool is often needed, and a damaged valve is a bigger problem.

The valve at the house is the one for you. If you cannot find it, call a plumber or your supplier before an emergency, not during one.

Types of main valves

Knowing the type tells you how to turn it.

Gate valve with a round wheel

Turn clockwise to close. Often stiff in older homes and can break if forced.

Ball valve with a lever

A quarter turn closes it. When the handle is across the pipe, it is closed.

Globe valve

A round wheel with a different seat. It slows flow more than a gate valve.

Meter box valve

A utility valve near the street. Leave it for the utility or a plumber.

No valve found

A missing or buried valve needs a plumber to locate or install one.

How to close and test the valve

Practice it once a year so you are not learning it in a flood.

Find it and clear the way

Move anything in front of it. Label it with a tag so anyone in the house can find it.

Turn it slowly

Turn a wheel clockwise or a lever a quarter turn. Stop if it feels like it will snap.

Open a low faucet

Draining the pipes through a faucet at the lowest point relieves the pressure.

Check that water stopped

Open another tap. If water still flows after a few seconds, the valve is not sealing.

Reopen slowly

Turn the valve back and let air purge from the taps before you use fixtures.

If the valve is stuck, leaking or broken

A valve that has not been turned in years can seize. Do not force it with a pipe wrench or a cheater bar. A valve that leaks around the stem may need a packing nut tightened or a repack. A valve that does not close fully needs replacement, and a full ball valve is a worthwhile upgrade.

When the valve fails during an emergency, you can close the utility valve at the meter box if you know how, or call the supplier. For City of Camarillo water customers, the Water Division is at (805) 388-5373. Our burst pipe repair team can replace a failing valve during the repair, and our water line repair page covers valves at the meter side.

Prepare your household

A few minutes of preparation protects your home.

  • Find the main valve and test it this month.
  • Tag it so everyone knows which one to turn.
  • Learn where the electrical panel is, in case you need to cut power near water.
  • Keep a flashlight and a phone charger near the valve.
  • Post our number near the valve: (805) 249-7135.

Fixture shutoffs and the water heater valve

Beyond the main, each toilet, sink and appliance should have its own stop valve. These let you close one fixture without cutting the whole house. Turn them yearly to keep them from seizing. If a stop valve will not close, replacing it is a small job, and our faucet and sink repair and toilet repair pages explain the details.

The water heater has its own cold supply valve at the top of the tank. In a heater leak, close that valve and the gas or power. See water heater replacement if the tank itself is leaking. In an emergency, shut off the main first, then work on the specific fixture.

Vacations, long absences and automatic shutoffs

If you leave the house for more than a few days, closing the main valve prevents a failed hose from flooding an empty home. Turn off the water heater or set it to vacation mode first, so the heater does not run without a supply. Some homeowners also install an automatic shutoff valve that closes when it senses a flow that does not stop, or a leak sensor that sends an alert to a phone.

These devices are a useful backup, but they do not replace knowing where the manual valve is.

Any automatic valve should go in on the house side of the meter, and it needs a clear space to work and a power source. We can install one with a new ball valve in the same visit. If you rent the house, ask the owner before you add equipment, and tell every adult in the home where the main valve sits.

Valve types and the code

The California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for a shutoff valve on the water service and at fixtures, and full port ball valves are widely used for that role. According to common practice, a valve that is turned once a year stays usable longer than one that is never touched. Ask us to replace a stiff gate valve before it fails.

Finding the valve in other towns

Valve locations vary by city and by builder. Oxnard homes are covered on our Oxnard page, and the areas page lists where we can help you find or replace yours.

Local note: who owns the valve in Camarillo

The meter and the box near the street belong to the water supplier. The valve at your house and the pipe past it are yours to maintain. For City of Camarillo water customers, the Water Division at (805) 388-5373 can answer questions about the meter side. Other areas are served by suppliers such as Camrosa Water District, so check your bill.

If you are at an address with a shared line or a unit in a complex, the main valve may be elsewhere, and the owner or association should know where it is.
