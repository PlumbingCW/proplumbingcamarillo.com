High Water Bill in Camarillo, CA: Finding a Hidden Leak

.

A bill that jumps with no change in habits usually means water is escaping. Most hidden leaks are in a toilet, an irrigation line or a supply pipe, and a few simple tests can tell you which. This guide walks through them in order.

Start with the meter

Your water meter is the best leak detector you own. Turn off every fixture in and around the house. No washing machine, no dishwasher, no sprinklers, no ice maker cycle. Then open the meter box and look at the leak indicator, a small dial or triangle that spins when any water moves through the meter. If it spins, water is moving, and something is leaking.

If your meter has no indicator, write down the numbers and wait an hour with everything off. If the reading changes, you have a leak. To find which side of the house it is on, close the main valve at the house and watch the meter.

If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house. For the second case, see water line repair.

Check the usual suspects first

Toilets are the leading hidden leak in most homes. A flapper that does not seal lets water trickle from the tank to the bowl all day. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing and see whether color shows up in the bowl. If it does, the flapper or fill valve needs service. Our toilet repair page covers that.

Irrigation is the second suspect. A broken sprinkler head, a cracked line or a stuck valve can run when the timer is off. Check the zone valves and look for wet spots in the yard. Then look at hose bibs, outdoor spigots and water softener or filter connections. A dripping faucet adds up too, and our faucet and sink repair team can fix it.

Signs of a leak you cannot see

A high bill is the symptom. These are the clues that point to the cause.

Meter running with all fixtures off

This is the clearest sign. Water is leaving the system.

Warm spots on the floor

A hot line under a slab can warm the concrete. See slab leak repair.

Musty smell or stained drywall

Moisture in a wall or ceiling often shows up in the finish first.

Sound of running water at night

Quiet makes small leaks audible.

Low pressure at a fixture

A leak ahead of the fixture takes flow with it.

Soggy yard patches with no rain

A leaking service line wets the soil.

How we find a leak that tests will not locate

When the meter test says there is a leak but the toilets and sprinklers are clean, we use pressure testing and electronic listening equipment. We isolate sections of the system with valves and see which section holds pressure. Then a ground microphone or a thermal camera can narrow the spot to a few feet.

The goal is a small hole in the wall or floor, not a large one. Our leak detection service explains the process, and we give a clear price before work starts. If the leak sits under a concrete floor, we talk through spot repair or a reroute, and our slab leak page covers those options.

Quick leak checklist

Work through these in order and you will narrow things quickly.

  • Write down the meter reading and the leak indicator state.
  • Run the toilet dye test on every toilet.
  • Check irrigation zones, hose bibs and outdoor spigots.
  • Look under sinks and around the water heater for drips.
  • Close the house main and watch the meter to split the problem in two.

What to do about the bill itself

Many water suppliers have a policy for leak adjustments. The rules vary and depend on proof of repair, so ask your supplier before you pay a bill that looks wrong. For City of Camarillo water customers, the Water Division number is (805) 388-5373. Keep the plumber's invoice and a note of the date the leak was fixed, since the supplier may ask for it.

Do not assume a leak is the only cause. A new pool, a filled tub, a change in irrigation schedule or extra people in the house will all raise a bill. Look at the pattern over several months, not just one cycle. If you need a hand reading the bill, we can walk through it on the phone.

Irrigation, pools and seasonal use

Not every spike is a leak. Outdoor water use can double a summer bill, especially with a lawn or a pool. Check your irrigation timer after a power outage, since some timers reset to a default schedule that waters every day.

A pool with an auto fill valve can add a lot of water if the pool has a slow leak or the valve sticks open. Compare your bill to the same month last year before you assume the worst.

If the pattern is steady, with the meter moving even at 3 in the morning, the leak is real and it is constant. If the usage is high only in the daytime, look at habits, guests, a new appliance or a dripping hose bib. A leak detection visit settles it when the tests are unclear, and we explain the result in plain terms.

Meters, units and how to read a bill

Water meters in homes usually read in cubic feet or gallons, and bills may use a unit of 100 cubic feet. According to your supplier's own bill format, check which unit is used before you compare months. A leak of one gallon a minute adds up to 1,440 gallons a day, which is why a running meter at night matters.

Bills and suppliers outside Camarillo

Local note: who bills your water

Not every Camarillo address buys water from the City of Camarillo. The Camrosa Water District serves part of the area around the city, and its potable supply is about half imported and half groundwater. Your bill names your supplier, and that is the office to call about meter readings and leak policies.

The City of Camarillo Water Division can be reached at (805) 388-5373. The meter and the line up to the meter belong to the utility, while the pipe from the meter to the house belongs to the owner, though you should confirm with your supplier for your address.
