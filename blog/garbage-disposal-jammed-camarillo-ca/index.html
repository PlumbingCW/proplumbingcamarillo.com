Garbage Disposal Jammed or Humming in Camarillo, CA

A disposal that hums but will not spin

A humming sound means the motor has power but the grinding plate cannot turn. Something is stuck between the plate and the shredder ring. It might be a fruit pit, a bone, a bottle cap or a bit of silverware. Turn the switch off first. Then turn the breaker off if you plan to reach into the unit, and never put your hand in the disposal.

Most disposals have a hex socket at the bottom. Fit the wrench that came with the unit, or a 1/4 inch hex key, and rock the plate back and forth until it moves freely. Use a flashlight and tongs to remove the object. Then press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit and test with water running.

Common disposal problems

The sound the disposal makes tells you a lot.

Hum and no spin

A jam. Free it with the hex key.

Silent with no hum

The reset has tripped or the outlet has lost power. Press the reset and check the breaker.

Leak from the bottom

A cracked housing or failed seal. Replacement is the usual fix.

Leak at the top flange

The sink seal or mounting ring has loosened. It can often be redone.

Slow draining sink after use

A clog in the drain line past the disposal.

Bad smell

Food residue sits in the grind chamber or the baffle.

What not to put in a disposal

A disposal grinds soft food scraps. It cannot handle everything. Bones, fruit pits, corn husks, celery strings, potato peels in large amounts, coffee grounds and eggshells are common causes of clogs and jams. Grease and oil are the biggest enemy, since they coat the drain line. The Camarillo Sanitary District code also bars un-shredded garbage from the sewer in Section 6002.

Run cold water while the disposal runs and for 15 seconds after. Cold water keeps fats solid so they are chopped and flushed instead of smeared. Feed scraps in small amounts. If a clog forms in the line after the disposal, see drain cleaning for the next step, and our guide to hydro jetting when it keeps returning.

How to reset and clear a jam safely

These steps clear most jams in a few minutes.

Turn off the switch and the breaker

Never reach into a disposal that could start.

Look with a flashlight

Use tongs or pliers to remove anything you can see.

Free the plate

Fit the hex key into the bottom socket and work it back and forth until it spins.

Press the reset

The red button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats.

Restore power and test

Run cold water, switch the disposal on and watch for leaks.

Installation and sizing

A new disposal ranges from a light duty 1/3 horsepower unit to a heavy 3/4 horsepower model that handles more load. Most homes do well with a mid-range unit. The mounting ring must match the sink, the drain must be clear and the dishwasher hose connects at the side port after the knockout plug is removed.

Skipping that plug is a common mistake that leaves the dishwasher with no drain path.

Smells, ice and other kitchen habits

A disposal smells when food residue sits on the shredder ring and the underside of the splash guard. Scrub the rubber guard with a brush and dish soap, then run cold water and a few citrus peels in small pieces.

Ice cubes can knock loose some residue, but they do not sharpen anything, and a disposal does not have blades in the way a blender does. It has impellers that fling food against a grind ring.

If a smell returns right away, the odor may come from further down the line, where grease and food have built up in the trap or the drain. A drain cleaning visit clears that, and a camera shows whether the line has a low spot. A sink that drains slowly after the disposal runs usually has a partial clog past the unit, not a disposal fault.

The dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drain hose joins the disposal at a small side port. On a new unit, a plastic knockout plug blocks that port and must be removed before the hose goes on. If the plug stays in, the dishwasher cannot drain, and water stands in the bottom after a cycle. If your dishwasher drains poorly after a replacement, check that plug first.

Local note: disposals and hard water

Camarillo well water averages 217.6 ppm hardness according to the City of Camarillo 2021 report. Scale can build up inside a disposal's drain and flange over time, and it can make the unit sound rougher. A vinegar and ice rinse once in a while can help a little, but scale on the housing is a sign of age.

If you have questions about what may go into the sewer from a home or a business, the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code is the reference. Section 6002 lists prohibited discharges, including un-shredded garbage.

Source: City of Camarillo 2021 water quality report and Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, Section 6002.
