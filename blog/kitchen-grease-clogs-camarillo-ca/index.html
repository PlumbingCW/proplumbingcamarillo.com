Kitchen Drain Grease Clogs in Camarillo, CA

.

Grease goes down the drain as a liquid and sets into a solid a few feet away. That is why a kitchen line can run fine for months and then back up all at once. This guide covers what causes it, what the local sewer rules say and how to clear and prevent it.

How grease builds a clog

Cooking fat and oil leave a pan warm and liquid. In a cool drain pipe, they cool, cling to the wall and collect food bits and soap. Layer by layer, the pipe narrows, and a dishwasher cycle or a hot rinse only pushes some of it further down. Eventually a spoonful of rice or a coffee ground finishes the job.

The pattern is a slow drain that comes and goes, a gurgle in the next sink and finally a full backup. A plunger may clear the trap, but the grease stays in the line and the clog returns. A cable can bore a hole through the buildup. Hydro jetting scours the walls and gives a cleaner result, which our hydro jetting page explains.

What the Camarillo Sanitary District rules say

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code lists what may not go into the sewer. Section 6002 prohibits liquids above 150 degrees Fahrenheit, un-shredded garbage, septic sludge and storm water, and it sets a 600 ppm limit on dispersed grease, oil and fat. Sections 16020 and 16030 cover grease control for food service businesses. The details are in the district code, so confirm what applies to your property with the district.

For a home, the practical rule is simple. Keep fats, oils and grease out of the drain. Wipe pans with a paper towel and put it in the trash, and pour cooled fat into a can that goes in the trash. Restaurants and other food service sites have their own rules, including grease trap or interceptor service, and our commercial plumbing page covers that side.

Prevention that works

The simplest tool is a paper towel and a trash can. Scrape plates, wipe pans and keep a can for cooled fat. Use a sink strainer to catch solids. Avoid running hot water to push grease down the drain, because it only moves the problem to a cooler section of pipe.

Enzyme cleaners and drain gels have mixed results and some can harm older pipe, so ask us before using them.

For a garbage disposal, the same rule applies. A disposal grinds food, but it does not make grease go away. Our garbage disposal repair page has more on what a disposal can and cannot handle. If your kitchen line backs up often, a camera inspection shows whether a belly or a root is part of the problem.

Restaurants, shops and shared lines

A business that cooks has a larger grease load than a home, and the district rules are stricter. Sections 16020 and 16030 of the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code cover grease control for food service, so a restaurant owner should read them or ask the district what applies to a specific site. A grease trap or interceptor needs regular cleaning, and a full trap lets grease pass into the sewer.

In a shared building, one tenant's grease can back up a neighbor's drain. Landlords and managers can call the same 24/7 line, and our commercial plumbing page covers service for kitchens, shops and offices. For repeat problems, we suggest a camera inspection of the shared line and a written cleaning schedule.

Dishwashers, soaps and the myth of hot water

Dish soap helps break up grease on a plate, but it does not make grease leave the pipe. Hot water does carry it further, and it cools in the line and sets again. That is why a kitchen drain that seems to work for a week can clog again a few feet downstream. Only mechanical cleaning, such as cabling or jetting, removes the layer.

Sewer code and the plumbing code on grease

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code sets the grease rules, and the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) sets how traps and vents work. A trap holds a water seal, and grease can coat it and slow the flow. According to the district code, Section 6002 limits dispersed grease, oil and fat to 600 ppm.

Local note: Camarillo sewer rules for grease

The Camarillo Sanitary District code sets a 600 ppm limit on dispersed grease, oil and fat in Section 6002 and lays out grease control rules for food service in Sections 16020 and 16030. Restaurants and similar sites in Camarillo should read those sections or ask the district what applies.

The district code also says the owner maintains the side sewer, including the lateral. That means a clog in your own line is yours to clear, while a blockage in the public main is for the district. A camera can show which one you have.

Source: Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, Sections 4107, 6002, 16020 and 16030.
