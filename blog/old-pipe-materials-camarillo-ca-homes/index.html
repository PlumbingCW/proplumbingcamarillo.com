Old Pipe Materials in Camarillo, CA Homes

.

The pipe in your wall tells you a lot about how it will age. Camarillo's housing stock grew fast from the 1960s through the 1990s, and several materials were common over those years. This guide helps you identify what you might have and what to watch for.

Why the building era matters

Camarillo incorporated in 1964. Its population was 2,359 in 1960, 19,219 in 1970, 37,797 in 1980 and 52,303 in 1990, per the census. That growth means thousands of homes went up in a few decades, built by different builders using whatever materials were common at the time. A home from the early 1970s may have a different system from one built in 1995.

You cannot date a pipe by guessing, so we identify it by sight, by test and by a look at the fittings. Knowing the material helps you decide whether to keep repairing or to plan a replacement. If you are thinking about the second option, see repiping for how a project runs.

How to tell pipe materials apart

A magnet helps. It sticks to galvanized steel and not to copper or plastic.

Hard water and how it treats each material

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. Scale builds in every pipe over time, and the narrowing is most obvious in galvanized steel, where rust and mineral deposits together can close a pipe to half its bore. Copper tends to resist scale better but can develop pinholes under certain conditions.

Plastic pipe does not rust or scale the same way, which is part of the reason PEX and CPVC became popular. They have their own limits. A softener can reduce scale in the lines downstream, and our water softener and filtration page covers what it does. For pressure problems, see water line repair.

Repair, monitor or replace

If the pipe is sound and the problem is one fitting, repair it. If the pipe is aging but has no leaks, monitor it and keep the shutoffs working. If the material has a failure record, or if leaks keep coming, plan a replacement. Partial repipes can handle one trouble spot, and a whole house repipe is a bigger project that gives a fresh system.

Insurance rarely covers a planned repipe. It may cover damage from a sudden leak, which is why a quick shutoff matters, and our burst pipe repair page explains what to do. For a permit, the City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division at (805) 388-5395 lists copper re-pipes among its online permit types.

If you are buying an older Camarillo home

A home inspection looks at plumbing, but it cannot see inside walls. Ask the seller what pipe material the house has, whether any part was replaced and whether there is a record of leaks or permits. Look at the water heater date, the main shutoff and the pipes under sinks.

Run every tap at once and watch for a drop in pressure. Open the cleanout cap, if you can find it, and ask for a camera look at the sewer lateral.

The sewer side matters as much as the supply side. The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code puts the side sewer, including the lateral, in the owner's hands, so a cracked or rooted lateral becomes your repair once you own the house.

A sewer camera inspection before closing gives you a record of its condition. For the supply side, a leak detection survey can find hidden leaks that an ordinary inspection misses.

Planning and budgeting a replacement

If the pipe needs replacement, plan the work around a remodel when you can, because open walls make the job easier and cheaper to finish. Ask for a written scope that names the material, the route and the wall repair.

Ask who pulls the permit. A partial repipe of the worst run can buy time while you save for a larger project, and our team can outline both options in the same visit.

Which codes apply to old pipe

Existing plumbing is judged by the code in force when it was installed, but new work follows the current California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A repipe or a major repair brings the changed section up to current code. According to the City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division, permit questions go to (805) 388-5395.

Local note: Camarillo growth and housing age

Camarillo grew from 2,359 people in 1960 to 70,741 in 2020. The biggest jumps came between 1960 and 1990, when the city went from about 2,400 to 52,303. Neighborhoods such as Mission Oaks in northeastern Camarillo and Leisure Village, which opened in 1973, belong to that period.

The age of your house is a starting point, not a verdict. Plumbing may have been replaced during a remodel, so the pipe in the wall may be newer than the home.
