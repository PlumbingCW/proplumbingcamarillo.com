If a pipe has burst right now

Find the main water shutoff, usually near the water meter, at the front of the house or in the garage. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the pressure. Switch off power to rooms with water on the floor if the breaker panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

Move belongings off the floor and take photos and video of the damage before you clean. Then call. If you cannot find the shutoff, the meter box at the street has a valve that the utility or a plumber can close. Do not use electrical items in wet areas, and keep children out.

What causes pipes to burst in Camarillo homes

Freezing is a less common cause in a mild climate. These are the ones we see more often.

Age and corrosion

Pipes that have thinned over decades fail suddenly under normal pressure.

High water pressure

A failed pressure regulator sends full street pressure into the house and finds the weakest point.

Failed fittings and connectors

Supply line braids, compression fittings and soldered joints can let go.

Ground movement or construction

Settling, digging and root pressure can crack lines in the ground.

Cold snaps

Hard freezes are uncommon here, but exposed pipes and outdoor hose bibs can still split in a cold night.

How we repair a burst pipe

We start with isolating the section and relieving pressure. Then we cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and the right fittings. For a copper run, we use proper couplings or soldered joints. For a PEX line, we use crimp or expansion fittings. After the repair, we restore water slowly and check every joint for leaks.

If the damage points to a larger problem, such as multiple pinholes or a corroded run, we say so and discuss repiping. If the burst is in the line between the meter and the house, see water line repair. For hidden leaks that remain after the repair, leak detection can find them.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

These steps limit damage and protect your claim.

  • Close the main shutoff and open a low faucet to relieve pressure.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel safely.
  • Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water.
  • Photograph and video the damage before cleanup.
  • Call your insurer to report the loss and ask about mitigation steps.

Insurance and documentation

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, though they often exclude the pipe repair itself and damage from long-term leaks. Take photos before you move anything, keep receipts for emergency repairs and ask your insurer what it requires for mitigation. We provide a written description of the repair for your file.

Pressure, valves and the code

Repair pipe and fittings follow the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and we test pressure before we close up. Many bursts trace back to high pressure, and the code sets 80 psi as the point at which a pressure reducing valve is required. If your reading is above that, ask us about a regulator.
