Why planned installs go better than emergency swaps

When the old tank is still working, you have time to choose the right unit. You can measure the closet, check the gas line size, and decide whether a different fuel type makes sense. In an emergency those choices shrink to whatever can be set today. A planned install lets us order the exact model and have the right parts on the truck.

If your heater is near the end of its life, an installation on your terms usually costs less than a scramble. If it is already failing, see our water heater replacement page, which covers the urgent case.

What an installation includes

A correct installation is more than setting a tank on a floor. The supply lines need shutoff valves and flexible connectors. Gas units need a proper gas connection, a sediment trap where required, and a vent that draws combustion gases outside. Electric units need correctly sized wiring and a disconnect. Every unit needs a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge line routed to a safe spot.

In a home with a closed plumbing system, such as one with a pressure regulator or backflow device at the meter, an expansion tank absorbs thermal expansion. Without one, repeated pressure spikes shorten the life of the tank and the fixtures. We check for this on every install.

Installation details we check on every job

Venting and combustion air

The flue must slope and seal correctly, and the room needs enough air for the burner.

Seismic bracing

Installers in California typically strap units so they stay upright during earthquakes. Ask that it be in the quote.

Drain pan and discharge line

In an attic or a finished space, a pan and drain line catch a future leak before it reaches the ceiling.

Expansion tank

Closed systems need one to protect the tank and the valves from pressure swings.

Shutoff valves

A working shutoff on the cold line above the heater makes the next repair a five-minute job.

Insulated hot lines

Insulating the first few feet of pipe cuts heat loss and shortens the wait for hot water.

Choosing between tank, tankless and hybrid

Our tankless water heater page covers the continuous hot water option and its gas and venting needs. A hybrid heat pump unit makes sense in homes with an electric supply and clear space around the heater. A conventional tank remains the simplest and often the fastest to install.

Camarillo hard water is a factor in every choice. The city reports well water averaging 217.6 ppm hardness, and scale shortens the life of elements and heat exchangers alike. Adding a softener or scale control, covered on our softener and filtration page, protects whichever unit you pick.

Codes and manufacturer rules for an install

A water heater install follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the maker's instructions. Together they cover venting, gas connections, the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe, a drain pan where needed and bracing. We check each item on every install and note it on the invoice, and the city permit and inspection confirm it.
