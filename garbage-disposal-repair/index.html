Leaking disposal: shut it down first

A disposal that leaks can soak the cabinet floor within minutes, and the unit connects to electrical power. Switch off the wall switch and, if you can, unplug the unit or turn off the breaker. Then place a bucket under the leak and call. Do not put your hand into the disposal for any reason.

The leak point tells us what failed. Water from the top flange means the sink seal has failed. Water from the side points to the dishwasher hose or the discharge pipe. Water from the bottom of the unit means the housing has cracked, and the unit needs replacing. The unit is a sealed machine, and a cracked housing cannot be repaired.

Disposal problems and likely causes

Disposals fail in a few predictable ways.

Hums but does not spin

The flywheel is jammed. A hex key in the base can often free it after the power is off.

Dead with no sound

A tripped reset button on the bottom or a tripped breaker. A failed motor is the next suspect.

Sink fills with water

A clog in the disposal outlet or the line past it. This needs drain cleaning.

Bad smell

Food buildup in the grinding chamber or the drain line.

Dishwasher backs up into the sink

The disposal inlet or the air gap is clogged.

Loud grinding or rattling

A foreign object in the chamber or a loose mount.

Repair or replace

A jam, a loose mount or a blocked outlet is a simple repair. A leaking flange is fixed by resetting the seal. A seized motor, a cracked housing or a unit that is past 8 to 12 years of use is usually better replaced. A new unit with a matching mount often goes in the same day.

We also install disposals where none exist, which requires a power source and a switch. For a kitchen remodel, see our plumbing installation page. We check the P-trap and the dishwasher connection at the same time, since those are the parts most likely to leak after a swap.

What goes down a disposal, and what the sewer district says

A disposal is meant for soft food scraps, not for grease, bones or fibrous peels. The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code limits dispersed grease, oil and fat in discharge to 600 parts per million, and it places the responsibility for blockages on the property owner's side sewer. Grease from a disposal ends up in the same line as everything else.

The practical rule is simple. Scrape fat into the trash, run cold water while the unit is on and flush for a few seconds after. If you notice repeat clogs, the line may need a hydro jetting visit to clear the grease buildup.

Before the plumber arrives

Safety first, then speed.

  • Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit or turn off the breaker.
  • Do not reach into the disposal chamber.
  • Place a bucket under any leak and clear the cabinet.
  • Note the brand and horsepower on the unit label.
  • Take a photo of where the water is coming from.

Where code applies to a disposal hookup

A disposal hookup follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The dishwasher drain normally connects above the trap, with a high loop or an air gap, so dirty water cannot flow back into the dishwasher. Local inspectors decide what they accept for a given job, and the City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division at (805) 388-5395 can answer permit questions.
