What we install

Most installation calls start with a remodel. A bathroom gets a new vanity and shower, a kitchen gets a new sink and dishwasher hookup, a laundry room moves to the other side of the house. Behind each of those are supply lines, drains, vents and valves that must be sized and routed correctly.

We install toilets, sinks, faucets, tubs, showers, dishwashers, washing machine boxes, hose bibs and water filtration points. We also handle new supply and drain lines for additions and accessory units. For water heaters, see water heater installation, which has its own page.

Common installation projects

Installation jobs vary, but the pattern is the same: plan the route, rough in, set the fixtures and test.

Bathroom remodel

New vanity, toilet, shower valve and drain. Rough-in comes before tile, and we coordinate timing.

Kitchen sink and dishwasher

Supply stops, a disposal and a dishwasher drain with the right air gap or high loop.

Laundry relocation

A new box with shutoffs, a standpipe and a trap, plus a drain that can handle the washer flow.

Outdoor hose bibs and irrigation tie-ins

A new bib needs a vacuum breaker and a way to drain.

Accessory dwelling or addition

New supply and drain connections tie into the existing system.

PEX piping: what it is and when we use it

PEX is flexible plastic tubing for hot and cold supply lines. It bends around obstacles, uses fewer joints than rigid pipe and installs faster in walls. It is a good fit for remodels and additions. It must be supported correctly and kept out of sunlight, and it should connect to fixtures with the right fittings.

Many homeowners ask whether PEX or copper is better. Both are accepted in residential work. We choose based on the route, the budget and the house. If you are weighing the question for an older home, see our repiping page.

Permits and inspections

Remodel plumbing often needs a permit. The City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division can tell you which category your project falls into, and its office line is (805) 388-5395. Online permit options include water heater replacements and copper re-pipes, and other work is handled through the same office.

A license is also part of the picture. The California Contractors State License Board says a license is required for any project valued at $1,000 or more in combined labor and materials. You can check any contractor at cslb.ca.gov or by calling 800-321-2752. We encourage you to verify every plumber you hire, including us.

Details that keep a new install trouble-free

Shutoff valves at every fixture make later repairs simple. Dishwasher drains need a high loop or an air gap. Washer standpipes need the right height and a trap. A water hammer arrestor on a quick-closing valve prevents banging in the pipes. These items cost little during installation and are hard to add later.

We also test under pressure and check every connection before the wall is closed. The City of Camarillo reports well water averaging 217.6 ppm hardness, so we recommend fixtures with accessible aerators and cartridges that can be cleaned. For fixture issues down the road, see faucet and sink repair.

Code and permits for new work

New fixtures and lines follow the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), along with the manufacturer's installation instructions. Pipe, fittings and valves must be listed for the use. Many jobs need a permit and an inspection, and we tell you which ones apply before we start.

Gas lines for appliances and what to do if you smell gas

Gas piping for a stove, dryer or outdoor fire feature is part of many remodels. It must be sized for the appliance, supported, tested for leaks and inspected, and it needs a permit in most cases. Ask the plumber to put the pipe size, the test and the inspection in the written scope, and to name who pulls the permit.

If you smell gas, do not touch a switch or use a phone inside. Leave the house, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside. After the utility makes it safe, call us for the repair. We do not diagnose gas leaks by guessing. A leak test with the right equipment is the only way to find one.
