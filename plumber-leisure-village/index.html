About Leisure Village

Camarillo's General Plan describes Leisure Village as a retirement community built in the 1970s, in the decade when the city's housing stock grew by about 61 percent. A community directory lists it as a 55 and older community with homes built from 1973 to 1984, mostly ranch style, and many of them duplexes, triplexes and quads.

That directory says its figures are not verified by the developer or the association, so treat the details as a starting point.

City-data mapping places it near US Route 101 and Conejo Creek, and its street addresses carry ZIP code 93012. The same directory lists a clubhouse and an 18-hole golf course. Because the homes are attached and managed by an association, plumbing work here follows a few rules that detached houses do not have.

Check the association first

A community directory reports that monthly dues cover items such as water, sewer, exterior upkeep and landscaping, though it says this is not verified. Dues vary by building, so read your own governing documents or call the association office before you book any work. A leak that starts in a shared wall or a shared line can also affect a neighbor, so tell management early.

Plumbing needs that fit attached, single-level homes

These are the calls that make sense for a community of this type. We are not saying any of them is more common here than elsewhere.

Water heater problems

No hot water, a pilot that will not stay lit or a tank that weeps at the base. Ask about the association's rules on venting and location before a swap. See water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Toilets that run or leak

Constant running, a rocking bowl or water around the base. See toilet repair and installation, including taller bowls that are easier to use.

Slow kitchen and bath drains

Cabling clears most single-fixture clogs. A backup in several fixtures points to the main line. See drain cleaning.

Unexplained wet spots or high bills

A damp wall or a meter that keeps moving with everything off. See leak detection.

Faucets and shutoff valves

Lever handles are easier on the hands than knobs, and old shutoff valves under sinks often stop closing. See faucet repair.

Garbage disposals

Humming, jamming or leaking at the flange. See garbage disposal repair.

Before you call a plumber in an association community

A few minutes of preparation saves time on the first visit.

Find your shutoff

Locate the shutoff for your unit and for the fixture. Ask the association where the building shutoff is.

Take photos

Photograph the leak or damage before cleanup. It helps with the association and with insurance.

Note who is affected

If water reached a neighbor's unit, write down which wall and which room.

Read the rules

Ask whether the association needs notice, approval or a licensed contractor for the work.

Water quality and the city's numbers

Camarillo's 2021 water report says the city draws about 26 percent of its water from city wells and about 74 percent from Calleguas Municipal Water District. Well water hardness averaged 217.6 ppm. Those are citywide figures, not numbers for Leisure Village. For your own supply, check which provider serves your address.

Hard water leaves scale on fixtures and inside water heaters, so a periodic flush and a rod check can matter. Our water softener and filtration page covers the options, and the association may have rules on equipment.
