Local water

Why hard water shapes plumbing in Camarillo

The 2021 city water report says Camarillo draws about 26 percent of its water from four city wells in the Fox Canyon Aquifer. About 74 percent comes from Calleguas Municipal Water District, which brings in State Water Project and Colorado River supply. The wells run harder than the imported water, and hardness shows up in water heaters, showerheads and old pipe.

We use those numbers as the starting point on service calls. A heater in a hard water home needs a flush and a rod check more often. A faucet that clogs every few months is probably scaled. A water softener can cut the problem at the source, and our tankless water heater page covers descaling.

Emergencies

What counts as an emergency

Call right away for sewage in a tub or floor drain, a leaking water heater, a burst pipe, a meter that runs with everything off or a toilet that will not stop overflowing. Close the nearest shutoff first. For a backed up main line, stop using water and find the cleanout. Our sewer line cleaning team clears the line and runs a camera when needed.

Not every call is an emergency. A dripping faucet, a slow drain or a planned fixture change can wait for a daytime visit. We tell you which is which on the phone, so you do not pay for urgency you do not need. For planned work, see plumbing installation and faucet and sink repair.
