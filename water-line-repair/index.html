Water line leaking right now: when the line from the meter fails

The water line carries all the water to your house, so a leak there is a steady loss. Signs include soggy ground in the yard, a bubbling spot near the meter, a driveway that sinks and a bill that rises. A full break can cut service altogether.

First, close the valve at the house and check the meter. If the meter still moves, the leak is in the line before the house valve. Note the location of any wet spot and call. If digging is involved, call 811 before anyone digs, because buried utilities lie along many routes.

Symptoms of a failing water line

A water line leak can be sudden or slow.

Wet or sunken ground

Water erodes soil along the line.

Meter moving with all fixtures off

The system is losing water somewhere.

Low pressure at every fixture

A leak ahead of the house, a partly closed valve or a failing regulator.

Discolored water after a pressure drop

Sediment in the line stirred up by flow changes.

No water at all

A complete break or a failed valve at the meter.

Repair or replace the line

A single break in a sound line can be repaired. If the line is old, has multiple leaks or has lost capacity to scale, replacement is the better long-term fix. We can replace a line by trenching or by trenchless methods that pull new pipe through the old route, which limits damage to landscaping and driveways.

We also check the pressure regulator, which controls pressure to the house. A failed regulator pushes high pressure into the system and shortens the life of every fixture. If other lines inside the house are failing, see repiping, and for a leak you cannot locate, leak detection.

Who owns what, and who to call

Responsibility for the line depends on where the utility's ownership ends, and that is a question for your supplier. For City of Camarillo water customers, the Water Division number is (805) 388-5373, and the report names it as the contact for water quality questions. Other suppliers may serve nearby addresses, so check your bill.

A supplier can confirm which side of the meter the leak is on. We can repair the owner side and coordinate with the utility on the rest. Digging in a public right-of-way may need permits, and the city can tell you what applies.

Pressure, regulators and the age of Camarillo homes

Camarillo grew from about 2,400 people in 1960 to over 52,000 in 1990, so many service lines are decades old. Pressure that is high at the street needs a regulator at the house. Pressure that has fallen over the years can point to scale or a narrowing line.

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, which supports scale in older steel and copper lines. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells us a lot in a minute. We bring one on every service call. For hot water pressure issues, see water heater repair.

Pressure rules and permits for the water line

The California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) sets 80 psi as the pressure above which a pressure reducing valve is required, so we test pressure on every water line call. Work in a public right-of-way may need a permit. For City of Camarillo water, the Water Division at (805) 388-5373 can say where the meter side ends.
