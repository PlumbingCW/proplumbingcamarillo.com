After-hours calls: what we handle for businesses

A plumbing failure at a business costs money by the hour. A restaurant cannot serve with a backed-up kitchen drain. A shop cannot open with water on the floor. A landlord gets calls from tenants at night. Our dispatch line is open around the clock for exactly these situations.

We handle drain and sewer backups, leaks, failed water heaters, running toilets, broken valves and fixture failures. When you call, tell us the type of property, whether water is still flowing and where the main shutoff is. If water is flowing, close the shutoff first and tell your staff to stay clear of wet electrical areas.

Common commercial calls

Kitchen drain and grease line backups

Food service lines carry more grease than a home, and they clog on a predictable cycle without maintenance.

Restroom fixture failures

Flush valves, toilets, urinals and sinks take heavy use, and they fail in ways that shut a restroom.

Water heater failures

A tank that supplies a kitchen or salon cannot be down for long.

Leaks in suites and common areas

A leak in one unit can reach another, and the source must be found quickly.

Landlord and property manager requests

Work orders from tenants often start as a symptom, and we trace the cause.

Grease, drains and what the Camarillo rules require

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code says a food service establishment may not discharge fat, oil or grease that could cause blockages. It also requires a grease control device that meets district standards and is maintained. The code also limits dispersed grease, oil and fat in discharge to 600 parts per million.

For a restaurant owner, this means grease control is a compliance matter as well as a plumbing one. We clear grease-laden drains with cable and hydro jetting and can camera the line afterward through our sewer inspection service. Grease trap pumping is handled by specialist haulers, and we coordinate rather than pretend to do it ourselves.

Backflow devices and why the supplier cares

A backflow device keeps water that has been in a building from flowing back into the public supply. Restaurants, irrigation systems and shops with chemical or boiler connections often have one. Your water supplier decides which devices need testing and how often, and it names who may do the test, so ask the supplier before you schedule anything.

We repair and replace backflow devices when they leak or fail a test. The test itself belongs to a certified tester on the supplier's list, and we can work with that tester so a failed device is fixed on the same visit. For City of Camarillo water, the Water Division at (805) 388-5373 can say what applies to your account.

Leak detection for shops, offices and rental buildings

A multi-unit building or a shop can lose a lot of water before anyone notices, because no single person watches the bill. Start with the meter test, then shut off sections and watch the meter again to split the building in two. Our leak detection team then listens for the leak in that section, so the repair is a small opening and not a wide one.

Rental owners should also check irrigation, shared hose bibs and each unit's toilets, because a running toilet in one unit looks like a building leak on the common bill.
