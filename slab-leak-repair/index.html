Slab leak right now: warm floors, running meters and rising bills

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs through or under a concrete foundation. The water does not show right away, because the concrete hides it. The signs appear over time. Floors feel warm, tile cracks, baseboards swell, carpet gets damp and you hear water running when nothing is on.

The meter is the first test. Turn everything off and watch the leak indicator. If it moves, water is leaving somewhere. Close the main valve at the house and watch again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house, and a slab leak is a strong possibility.

What a slab leak looks like

Most slab leaks announce themselves by heat, sound or a bill.

Warm or hot spot on the floor

A leaking hot water line heats the concrete above it.

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle when all fixtures are off.

Cracks and lifted flooring

Water under the slab can lift or crack tile and wood.

Damp carpet or baseboard

Moisture rises through the slab and into the finish.

Rising water bill

A hidden leak runs 24 hours a day.

Low water pressure

Lost flow shows up at the fixtures downstream.

How we find it and fix it

We start with the meter and pressure tests to confirm the leak and decide which line. Then we use electronic listening equipment and thermal imaging to pinpoint the spot. Marking the exact location keeps the cut small, and the cut is the part that damages your floor.

Three repair paths exist. A spot repair cuts through the slab at the leak and replaces the damaged section. A reroute abandons the leaking line and runs new pipe overhead through walls and attic, which avoids slab work.

Lining coats the inside of the pipe and suits some lines but not all. The right choice depends on pipe age, leak location and floor finish. We explain each one and the price before we begin.

There is no single best answer.

Why slab leaks are a Camarillo topic

Camarillo grew rapidly between the 1960s and the 1990s, and houses built on concrete slabs from that period often have supply lines running under or through the foundation. Those lines are decades old now. The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, which leaves mineral scale in older pipes and on fittings.

We cannot say that hard water causes slab leaks, and we do not claim it. We can say that older pipe, high pressure and hard fittings are a common mix in this age of home. Checking your pressure regulator is an easy preventive step, and it ties into our repiping and water line repair work.

What to do right away

These steps protect the house and your claim.

  • Run the meter test with all fixtures off.
  • Close the main valve if you see water or hear it running.
  • Do not drill or cut the floor yourself.
  • Photograph damp or warm spots and note where they are.
  • Call your insurer and ask about coverage for leak detection and damage.

Reroutes, cuts and the code

A reroute or a spot repair follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Pipe is listed for potable water, supported and tested before it is covered. The City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division at (805) 388-5395 can say whether a given repair needs a permit.
