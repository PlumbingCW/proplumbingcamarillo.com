What the camera tells you

A camera shows pipe material, joint condition, cracks, roots, offsets, bellies where water pools, scale and grease buildup. The operator marks distance from the entry point and uses a locator to find the surface position of anything of interest. That position matters, since it limits digging to the right spot.

You get a recording and a summary of findings. The recording also protects you in a purchase or a dispute, because it documents the condition on the day it was made. We note the pipe diameter and material, and we flag anything that needs action or monitoring.

When to schedule an inspection

An inspection is a planned job in most cases, though it often follows an emergency.

Before buying a home

A line that looks fine on paper may have roots or a belly. The video lets you negotiate with facts.

After a backup

Clearing the clog is only half the job. Knowing why it clogged saves a repeat.

Before a remodel or landscaping

A new patio or tree should not sit over a failing line.

When fixtures gurgle or smell

The line may be partially blocked or leaking gas into the yard.

Before choosing a repair method

Lining, spot repair and replacement each need the pipe's actual condition to be priced correctly.

What happens during the visit

We locate the cleanout and clear any loose debris so the camera has a clean view. The camera head, mounted on a push cable, goes down the line while we watch the monitor and record. If the line is fully blocked, we clear it first with a cable or hydro jetting so the camera can pass.

We pause at each issue and mark its depth and distance. At the end, we pull the camera, review the footage with you and answer questions. If the findings point to a repair, we outline the options and the price. If the line is sound, we say so and give you the record.

Buying a house in Camarillo? Inspect the lateral

Camarillo homes from the 1960s through the 1990s sit on laterals that may be original. The city's population grew from about 2,400 in 1960 to over 52,000 by 1990, so a large share of the housing stock is old enough that the sewer pipe deserves a look. A home inspection rarely includes the lateral.

Under the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, the property owner maintains the side sewer, including the lateral to the district main. A buyer who inherits a failing line pays for it. An inspection before closing gives you the video to negotiate a repair credit or a price adjustment. For homes in Oxnard, see our Oxnard page.

What we do not promise

A camera cannot see through a fully collapsed pipe or past a major offset. If the camera cannot pass, we say what it did see and recommend sewer line repair or locating work. We also do not diagnose a line we have not run, and we do not guess at its condition from the cleanout cap.

Findings are a snapshot. Roots grow, joints shift and a line that looks fine now can change in a few years. If your line is older and has trees nearby, a recheck every few years is reasonable. Regular cleaning with sewer line cleaning keeps flow steady between checks.

What the camera record is measured against

A camera shows the pipe, and the standards decide what the video means. The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, according to its sections on side sewers, makes the owner responsible for the lateral and requires a cleanout. We note the distance from the cleanout to each defect and describe it in plain terms, so the record is useful to a city, a district or a buyer.
