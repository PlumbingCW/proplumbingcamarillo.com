Camarillo, our home base

We are at 2400 Las Posas Road, Camarillo, CA 93010. Camarillo incorporated in 1964, grew from 2,359 residents in 1960 to 52,303 in 1990 and counted 70,741 in 2020. The city has ZIP codes 93010 and 93012 for street addresses, and some ZIP codes in the city are post office box only. We serve addresses in all of Camarillo, and the full range of our plumbing services is available.

Camarillo is also home to California State University Channel Islands, which opened in August 2002 on the former Camarillo State Hospital site, and to Camarillo Airport, the former Oxnard Air Force Base that closed in 1970. Mission Oaks in northeastern Camarillo and Leisure Village, opened in 1973, are two neighborhoods where the housing dates from the growth decades.

Oxnard

Oxnard is the large city to the west, with 202,063 residents in 2020. Its street ZIP codes are 93030, 93033, 93035 and 93036. We reach Oxnard from our Camarillo base, and our Oxnard plumbing page has the local water and sewer facts, including the city's 2023 hardness range and who owns the sewer lateral.

Which water and sewer rules apply to you

Water suppliers differ across the area. Camarillo is served by the City of Camarillo and, in places, by Camrosa Water District, whose potable supply is about half imported and half groundwater. Sewer service also differs by address. The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code covers the sanitary district, and the City of Oxnard covers its own wastewater system.

Your water bill names your supplier. That is the office to call about meters, leaks and water quality. For City of Camarillo water, the Water Division is at (805) 388-5373. For permits, the City of Camarillo Building and Safety Division is at (805) 388-5395.

Why the local history matters to a plumbing call

The dates above are not trivia. A house built in the 1960s or 1970s may still have its original water heater location, its first supply lines and a lateral that is half a century old. We use the age of the neighborhood to decide what to check first, which is why we ask the build year on the phone.
