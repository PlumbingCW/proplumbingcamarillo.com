A main line backup is not a normal clog

A kitchen sink clog is a nuisance. A main line backup is an emergency, because every fixture in the house drains to the same pipe. The signs are clear. The toilet gurgles when the washer drains. The lowest tub or floor drain fills with water. More than one fixture is slow at the same time.

Stop using water and call. Every gallon added makes the backup worse, and the lowest drain in the house is where it will spill first. If sewage reaches the floor, keep children and pets away and leave electrical items alone until the area is dry.

How we clear a main sewer line

We find the cleanout and run a heavy cable machine through the line. The cutting head grinds through roots, breaks up grease and punches through solids. We then follow with hydro jetting when the line has buildup that a cable leaves behind. A final camera pass shows whether the line is truly clear and why it clogged.

If there is no cleanout, we work through a vent or by pulling a toilet. That takes more time, and we recommend installing a cleanout so future cleanings are quick. The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code requires a cleanout at the point where the building sewer meets the lateral, so a new cleanout also keeps your system in line with the code.

What usually blocks a main line

Cleaning works best when we know the cause. These are the usual suspects:

Roots

Fine roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper and waste.

Grease

Fat and oil from kitchen drains coat the pipe and harden over years.

Flushed items

Wipes, feminine products and paper towels do not break down.

A belly or offset

Low spots hold water and solids, and the line clogs again soon after cleaning.

Broken pipe

A cracked section lets soil and roots in.

Local rules that affect sewer cleaning in Camarillo

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code states that side sewers are maintained by the owner of the property, so cleaning the lateral is the owner's responsibility. The code also limits what may enter the sewer. It lists liquids above 150 degrees Fahrenheit, storm water and septic tank sludge among prohibited discharges.

It limits dispersed grease, oil and fat to 600 parts per million and requires food service businesses to control grease. If you manage a restaurant, see our commercial plumbing page. For repeated trouble that points to a pipe fault rather than buildup, see sewer line repair.

How to keep the line clear between visits

A few habits make a large difference. Wipe pans before washing, so grease goes in the trash and not the drain. Flush only toilet paper. Keep trees with aggressive roots away from the lateral, and know where your cleanout is so a plumber can reach it fast.

Cleanouts, discharge rules and the code behind them

Cleanouts are a code item under the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and a district requirement under Section 4105 of the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code. A cleanout lets us clean the line without pulling a fixture. We also keep to the district rules on what may be discharged, so wastewater from a cleaning goes where it should.
