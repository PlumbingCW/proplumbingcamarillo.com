What the city report says about hardness

The City of Camarillo's 2021 annual water quality report lists three sources. The city's own well water, from four wells in the Fox Canyon Aquifer, made up about 26 percent of supply, and it averaged 217.6 ppm hardness with a range of 101 to 350 ppm.

Calleguas Municipal Water District water, about 74 percent of supply, was reported at 122 ppm from the Jensen plant and 272 ppm for locally stored surface water that Calleguas treated.

In grains per gallon, divide parts per million by 17.1. That puts the city well average near 12.7 grains per gallon, the Jensen figure near 7.1 and the stored surface water near 15.9. Laboratories commonly describe water above 180 ppm as very hard. The mix at your tap depends on where you live, so test your tap and check your supplier's latest report. Your water bill names your supplier.

Signs that hard water is costing you

White crust on faucets and showerheads

Calcium and magnesium dry on surfaces and leave scale.

Spots on dishes and glass

Minerals leave film as water dries.

Short water heater life

Scale on elements and tank bottoms lowers efficiency and shortens life. See water heater repair.

Dry skin and stiff laundry

Soap reacts with minerals and leaves residue.

Low flow at fixtures

Scale clogs aerators, valves and showerheads.

Softener, conditioner or filter: what each does

A salt-based softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium through ion exchange, so water leaves the tank soft. A salt-free conditioner changes how minerals form crystals so they do not stick as readily, but it does not lower hardness. A whole-house carbon filter removes chlorine taste and odor. A reverse osmosis unit under the sink produces drinking water with most dissolved solids removed.

These tools do different jobs. A softener protects pipes and appliances from scale. A filter improves taste. Reverse osmosis improves drinking water. Many homes in Camarillo benefit from a softener, and some add a drinking water filter. We recommend based on your test results and your goals, not on a standard package.

Installation and service

A softener needs a bypass valve, a drain connection and a power outlet. We install it on the main line after the hose bibs you do not want softened, and set the hardness and capacity. We also service existing systems, replace valves and tanks, and sanitize the resin bed on request.

For tankless owners, softening is part of heat exchanger protection. See tankless water heater service for the descaling schedule. For fixtures that are already scaled, our faucet repair team replaces cartridges and aerators.

Questions to ask before you buy a system

Good questions prevent bad purchases.

  • What is the hardness at my tap, tested at the home?
  • How much salt will it use, and how often do I refill?
  • Where will it drain, and does the drain meet code?
  • Do any local rules limit softener discharge? Ask your water supplier.
  • What service does it need, and who services it?
