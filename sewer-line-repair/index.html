Sewer backup: what to do in the first hour

A sewage backup is a health hazard as well as a mess. Stop running every fixture, including the washer and dishwasher, because each gallon you add has nowhere to go. Keep children and pets out of the room, and do not walk through standing sewage in bare feet. If the water is near outlets, leave the breaker for that area off.

Take photos of the water line and affected items for any insurance claim, then call. We start by clearing the line enough to stop the backup, and then run a camera to see what caused it. Clearing alone is a temporary fix when the real problem is a break, a belly or roots in a joint.

What a failing sewer line looks like

Some sewer problems announce themselves. Others show up as a pattern over months.

Repeated backups in the lowest drain

A shower or floor drain at the lowest point is the first to overflow when the main is partially blocked.

Gurgling toilets and slow drains in several rooms

Air is being pushed through the system because water cannot pass a restriction.

Sewer smell in the yard

A leaking joint or crack lets gas and water escape into the soil.

Green, lush patch in the lawn

Sewage fertilizes grass. A strip that grows faster than the rest can mark a leak.

Sinkhole or soft ground near the line

Water erodes soil around a broken pipe and can undermine the surface.

Repair paths: spot repair, lining or replacement

A short crack or a single offset joint can be fixed with a spot repair.

We expose the pipe, cut out the damaged section and set new pipe with proper couplings. A longer run with multiple cracks or heavy root intrusion is often better served by pipe lining, a trenchless method where a resin-saturated liner is pulled through the pipe and cured to form a new pipe inside the old one.

When the pipe has collapsed, lost its slope or has a belly that holds water, lining does not help, and replacement is the sound answer. Our camera inspection decides which path fits. We show you the footage and explain the options in plain terms, then you choose.

Who owns the line, and which permits apply

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code defines the lateral as the portion of sewer that connects the building sewer to the main public sewer. It says side sewers are maintained by the owner of the property they serve.

In practice, repairs to the pipe from the house to the district main are a homeowner expense. The same code requires a written district permit to construct a building sewer or lateral sewer.

That means a repair that goes beyond a small spot fix should involve the district, and we help you understand what to ask. Properties in Oxnard follow that city's rules. The City of Oxnard says the property owner is responsible for the private sewer lateral, including the connection to the public sewer in the right-of-way, so see our Oxnard plumber page if that is your address.

Roots, age and the 1960s through 1990s housing boom

Much of Camarillo's housing went up between the mid 1960s and the 1990s. The city grew from about 2,400 residents in 1960 to more than 52,000 in 1990. Sewer laterals from that period are decades old, and joints in older pipe can open slightly over time. Roots enter through those joints in search of moisture and fill the pipe.

Trees near the lateral are the most common cause we see. A tree with a vigorous root system within a few feet of the line will find a leak sooner or later. We note where the roots entered on camera, since a repair that leaves the joint open will fail again. Clearing with a hydro jetting pass between repairs can maintain flow while you plan.

Pipe standards and permits for a lateral

Replacement pipe follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with listed pipe, the right slope and a cleanout. The Camarillo Sanitary District requires a written permit to build a lateral under Section 4108. We confirm permits and inspections with the district before we dig, and we describe each step in the written scope.
