Overflowing toilet: stop the water first

The fastest way to stop an overflow is the shutoff valve on the wall behind the toilet. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If the valve will not turn, lift the tank lid and hold the flapper down or lift the float to cut the fill. Then mop and move rugs, and do not flush again.

If more than the toilet is backing up, such as a tub or sink, the clog is past the toilet, and our drain cleaning team should handle it. A single clogged toilet can often be cleared with a closet auger. We bring one, and we also check whether the toilet is set right.

Toilet problems and their usual causes

Most toilet trouble falls into a handful of causes.

Running toilet

A worn flapper, a misadjusted fill valve or a chain that is too short lets water leak into the bowl. It wastes water and raises the bill.

Water at the base

A failed wax ring or loose closet bolts let water escape at the floor. This leak can rot the subfloor.

Weak flush

A partial clog in the trap or mineral scale in the rim holes cuts flush power.

Phantom flushing

A slow leak from the tank to the bowl makes the fill valve refill repeatedly.

Wobbly toilet

Loose bolts or a damaged flange. Rocking breaks the seal, so address it soon.

Tank sweating or leaking

Condensation is harmless. A leak at the tank bolts or the supply line needs repair.

Repair or replace a toilet

If the problem is a flapper, fill valve or wax ring, repair is simple and inexpensive. We replace those parts and test the flush. If the porcelain is cracked, the toilet is old and inefficient or the bowl keeps clogging, a new toilet makes more sense.

A new toilet should be set on a sound flange with a fresh wax ring or gasket and secured with the right bolts. A flange that is cracked or below the floor level needs a repair kit before the toilet goes back. Taking short cuts there leads to a leak months later. For a new fixture in a remodel, see our plumbing installation page.

Hard water and your toilet in Camarillo

The City of Camarillo reports well water hardness averaging 217.6 ppm, with a range of 101 to 350 ppm. Minerals leave scale on fill valves, flappers and the rim jets that wash the bowl. Scale on the flapper seat is a common reason a flapper stops sealing, and clogged rim jets reduce flush power.

If your toilet runs after you replace the flapper, scale on the seat may be the cause. We clean the seat and replace worn parts. For a larger fix, the water treatment options on our softener and filtration page reduce scale throughout the house.

Before the plumber arrives

A few steps limit damage until the plumber arrives.

  • Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • Do not flush again, and do not pour chemicals into the bowl.
  • Mop up water and move rugs away from the toilet.
  • Take a photo of any leak at the base for the visit.
  • If the shutoff will not close, close the main water valve at the house.

What the plumbing code says about toilet installs

A new toilet follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) for clearances, the connection to the drain and the supply. The flange must sit on a solid floor, and the seal must make full contact. If a repair reveals a damaged flange or floor, we show you the problem and explain the fix before we reset the toilet.
