When a clogged drain is an emergency

A slow drain can wait for the next appointment. A drain that has stopped completely cannot, especially when the toilet gurgles after the washing machine drains or when several fixtures back up at once. Those signs point to a blockage in the main line rather than in one fixture, and the water has nowhere to go but up.

Our line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays, and we book same-day appointments when we can. While you wait, do not flush, do not run the dishwasher or washer, and do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a drain that is already full. Chemical cleaner sitting in standing water burns skin and makes the plumber's work harder and less safe.

What the clog is usually telling you

Where the water shows up says a lot about where the clog sits. A few patterns we check first:

Only one fixture is slow

The blockage is in that fixture's own trap or branch line. This is the easiest clog to clear and rarely needs more than a cable through the trap arm.

Two or more fixtures back up together

The blockage sits downstream of where those lines join. A bathtub that gurgles when the toilet is flushed is the classic pattern.

Water comes up in the lowest drain

A shower or floor drain at the lowest point of the house shows a main line problem first, because that is where backed-up water exits.

The clog returns within weeks

Something is holding the debris, such as roots, a belly in the pipe or years of grease. Clearing it again without finding the cause only buys time.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen sinks clog because of grease, food scraps and soap residue that cool and harden inside the trap and the horizontal branch behind the wall. In a house with a garbage disposal, the disposal outlet and the first few feet of drain take the worst of it.

If the sink is clear but the dishwasher drains slowly, the fault is often at the disposal inlet. Our garbage disposal repair team can look at both in one visit.

A small hand cable handles most kitchen branch lines. When grease has lined the pipe over many years, a cable only punches a hole through it, and the sink will clog again within a month or two. In that case we recommend hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall instead of just opening a channel.

Drain snaking and cabling: what we use and when

Snaking means passing a rotating steel cable through the pipe so the head cuts or hooks the obstruction. For sinks and tubs we use smaller cables with a cutting head. For laundry standpipes and larger branches we step up to a heavier cable. For a main line we bring a machine with enough cable length to reach the cleanout and well past it.

The cable also tells us something. If it stops hard at the same distance every time, we suspect a broken joint or an offset. If it comes back with a fibrous mat on the head, roots are involved. In either case we recommend a sewer camera inspection so the repair decision rests on a picture of the pipe.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of preparation protects floors and speeds up the work.

  • Stop using water in the house, including the dishwasher and washing machine.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a drain that is holding water.
  • Find the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the house foundation, and clear the area around it.
  • Move rugs and stored items away from the lowest drain in case it overflows.
  • Take a photo of any water that has come up, since it helps if an insurance claim follows.

Why Camarillo drains clog the way they do

Most of Camarillo grew between the mid 1960s and the 1990s. The city incorporated in 1964, and the population went from about 2,400 in 1960 to more than 52,000 by 1990. Homes from that stretch often have the original sewer lateral, and the pipe under a front yard has been taking roots and settling for decades.

A mature tree near the lateral is the most common reason we see a main line clog return.

Under the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code, the property owner maintains the side sewer that serves a building, which includes the lateral up to the district main. The same code requires a cleanout at the point where the building sewer meets the lateral.

Code items we check on a drain call

Drain work follows the California Plumbing Code (CPC), which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Traps, vents and cleanouts are code items, so we confirm a cleared drain still has a working trap and a clear vent before we leave. On the sewer side, the Camarillo Sanitary District operations code decides what may enter the system, and Section 6002 lists the prohibited discharges.
