About Old Town Camarillo

A local tourism source describes Old Town as shops and restaurants along Ventura Boulevard, with St. Mary Magdalen Church at 2532 Ventura Boulevard, built in 1914. A local real estate blog quotes the city's Housing Element calling the area the original commercial district, running along Ventura Boulevard between Lewis Road and Carmen Drive, with the Metrolink station on Lewis Road. Camarillo itself incorporated in 1964.

Street addresses on Ventura Boulevard use ZIP code 93010. We could not confirm a formal boundary, so we describe Old Town by its main street. Homes sit near the district, but we describe the district itself as commercial and mixed use.

Grease and the Camarillo Sanitary District code

The Camarillo Sanitary District operations code prohibits certain discharges under Sec. 6002 and sets a 600 ppm limit for grease. Secs. 16020 and 16030 cover food service establishments. A restaurant should know the grease rules for its address before it plans maintenance or a remodel.

Regular cleaning of kitchen lines and any grease interceptor is the practical response, and it costs less than an emergency backup. For a line that keeps filling, sewer camera inspection shows what is in it. Ask the district which rules apply to your property.

Small commercial buildings and nearby homes

Landlords and tenants should agree in writing who calls and pays for plumbing work. Our commercial plumbing page explains the service calls we take for shops, offices and restaurants.

A small shop or office may have one restroom group and a utility sink, so a single failed fixture can close the space for a day. Ask your landlord or property manager for a simple note of where the shutoffs and cleanouts are, and keep a copy at the front counter. Knowing that before something fails saves time when you call.

Homes near Old Town are part of Camarillo, so everything on our main Camarillo plumbing page applies to them. For a residential neighborhood example, see Mission Oaks. Our service areas page lists the rest.

Before you call for a business plumbing problem

  • Tell us whether the building is owned or leased.
  • Note which fixtures are affected and when it started.
  • Close nearby shutoffs if water is spreading.
  • Keep receipts for any earlier plumbing work.
  • Keep the kitchen records that show grease cleaning dates.
